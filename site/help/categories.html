Categories

A category is a classification you assign to a transaction so you can see, later, what your money was for.

How categories work

You decide what your categories are. Common ones are Groceries, Rent, Salary, Petrol, Eating Out. Each transaction can carry one category, and a split transaction can carry a different category on each leg.

Categories form a tree. A category can have a parent, which lets you group related ones together — for example, Groceries as a parent of Supermarket and Markets. Reports roll the children up under the parent, so you can drill from the broad picture into the detail.

Each category's place in the tree comes from its Parent field. A category with no parent sits at the top level; setting a parent nests it underneath. The parent picker shows your existing categories as a tree, and a category can't be its own ancestor — the picker won't let you pick a descendant as the parent.

Where categories show up

Categories drive the Reports view, where you see income and expense by category for any date range. They also feed the expense breakdown in Analysis, and they're what budgets inside an earmark are measured against.

If you've never set any up, transactions are still fine without a category — they won't appear in the by-category totals, but everything else works.
