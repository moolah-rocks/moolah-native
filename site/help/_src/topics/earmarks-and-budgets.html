Earmarks and budgets

Earmarks are how you set money aside in Moolah without splitting it out into a separate account. This article explains what an earmark is and how a budget fits inside one.

What an earmark is

An earmark is a named bucket of money that sits on top of your accounts. The cash still lives in your chequing or savings account; you've reserved part of it for a specific purpose. Common uses are a holiday, a tax bill, or the next car service.

Earmarks aren't accounts. They don't add to your total balance; they shade part of it as already spoken for. The sidebar shows the relationship as Available Funds, which is your Current Total minus your Earmarked Total.

Saving toward a goal

An earmark can have a savings goal: a target amount and an optional start and end date. The earmark's detail view shows a progress bar against that target, so you can see how close you are.

A savings goal is a property of the earmark, not a separate kind of object. You add it when you create the earmark or any time afterwards from Edit Earmark.

Budgeting per category

An earmark can also hold a budget: planned spending broken down by category. The Budget tab on the earmark detail shows each category line item alongside the actual amount you've spent against it.

Budgets are useful when an earmark covers an ongoing area — say, a monthly grocery budget — rather than a one-off goal. You can use a savings goal, a budget, both, or neither on the same earmark.
