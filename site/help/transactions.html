Transactions

A transaction is a single line in your money history — the day-to-day stuff that adds up to your balances, reports, and totals.

What a transaction holds

Each transaction has a date, a payee, an amount, and a type. You can also add a note, a category, and an earmark. Behind the scenes, a transaction is made of one or more legs, where each leg ties to one account. Most everyday transactions only have one leg, so you rarely have to think about that detail.

The five types you'll pick

The Type picker on a transaction sets its shape:

  • Income — money coming in, like a salary, interest, or freelance payment.
  • Expense — money going out, like groceries or a subscription.
  • Transfer — money moving between two of your own accounts.
  • Trade — buying, selling, or swapping one currency or asset for another. Stock and crypto trades both live here.
  • Custom — a multi-leg shape for splits and other arrangements that don't fit the four above.

You'll also see Opening Balance on accounts when you create them. Moolah writes that one for you and you can't edit it.

A note on signs

Expenses are usually negative and income is usually positive, but the sign reflects what actually happened. A refund is an expense with a positive value. On a credit card, the balance owed is a negative number. Transfer and trade legs keep the signs you enter — Moolah doesn't flip them for you.
