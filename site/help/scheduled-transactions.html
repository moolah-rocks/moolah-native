Scheduled transactions

A scheduled transaction is a future-dated transaction in Moolah — useful for bills, rent, subscriptions, and any other money movement you know is coming.

Scheduled and recurring

Every scheduled transaction has a date in the future. Some happen only once, like a one-off invoice you'll pay next week. Others repeat — your phone bill, your rent, your monthly transfer to savings.

The difference lives in the Recurrence section of the transaction:

  • Once — scheduled, but not recurring. It appears in your upcoming list until you pay it, then it leaves.
  • Day, Week, Month, or Year — recurring, with an "every N" multiplier. You can set up "every 2 weeks", "every month", "every 3 months", and so on.

Moolah describes the schedule back to you in plain English (for example, "Every 2 weeks") so you can confirm at a glance.

Moolah doesn't post them for you

This is the most important thing to know: Moolah does not post scheduled transactions automatically. The amount stays out of your real balance until you mark it paid.

When a scheduled transaction comes due, you confirm it with Pay Now. That's deliberate. Real life rarely matches a schedule exactly. The rent goes up, the direct debit runs a day late, the streaming service charges a different amount. Pay Now lets you adjust the date and amount to what actually happened, then record it.

For a recurring transaction, paying it advances the schedule to the next occurrence. For a one-off, paying it removes it from the scheduled list.

Where you see them

Scheduled transactions show up in a few places:

  • The Scheduled sidebar item lists everything that's still pending.
  • The Upcoming view (⌘2 on Mac) groups what's due soon.
  • The Analysis view uses them to project your forecast.

They don't appear in your main account balance until you pay them, but they do feed forecasts and projections so you can see what's coming.
